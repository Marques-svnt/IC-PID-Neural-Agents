\begin{abstract}
Deploying fixed-gain Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) controllers on non-isothermal Continuous Stirred Tank Reactors (CSTRs) presents critical challenges stemming from Arrhenius kinetic non-linearities, steady-state multiplicity, and hard actuator constraints. This paper presents an exhaustive, rigorous benchmark investigating the physical controllability boundaries, anti-windup mechanics, and robustness trade-offs of classical PID tuning paradigms. Four representative strategies—Ziegler--Nichols (ZN), Cohen--Coon (CC), Skogestad Simple Internal Model Control (SIMC), and Constrained Numerical Optimization (ITAE with an $\mathcal{H}_\infty$ sensitivity constraint $M_s \le 1.6$)—are systematically evaluated on a high-fidelity three-state CSTR model subject to cooling valve amplitude saturation ($[0, 300]$~L/min) and electro-pneumatic slew-rate dynamics. Our findings reveal that empirical reaction-curve rules (ZN and CC) predictably fail on lag-dominant exothermic reactors ($\theta/\tau \approx 0.085$): excessive proportional action immediately saturates the actuator, provoking destructive limit cycles and near-runaway thermal excursions ($\Delta T > 43$~K) under load disturbances. Conversely, model-based SIMC ($\tau_c = 0.40$~min) and Constrained Optimal ITAE demonstrate superior closed-loop resilience, maintaining smooth damping ($M_p \le 12\%$, $\text{IAE} < 1.8$) and preserving stability under heat exchanger fouling down to $70\% UA$. At $60\% UA$, physical cooling limits establish a hard thermodynamic controllability boundary where linear control authority collapses. Furthermore, by sweeping the closed-loop design parameter $\tau_c$ and executing scalarized multi-objective optimization, we construct the authentic continuous Pareto frontier between tracking accuracy ($\text{IAE}$) and actuator physical wear ($\text{TV}$), delineating the quantitative boundary between high performance and actuator degradation. This benchmark establishes an authoritative empirical baseline for subsequent investigations into adaptive neural networks and autonomous multi-agent supervisory architectures.
\end{abstract}
