\subsection{Nominal Setpoint Tracking Benchmark}
The four classical PID tuning strategies were first evaluated under nominal operating conditions for a $-2.0$~K step change in reactor temperature setpoint ($T_{sp} = 394.65$~K) under realistic sensor noise ($\sigma = 0.05$~K). The closed-loop state and control trajectories are illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:classical_benchmark}, with quantitative performance metrics summarized in Table~\ref{tab:classical_benchmark}.

    \label{fig:classical_benchmark}
\end{figure}

\input{tables/benchmark_table}

The simulation highlights critical trade-offs in classical tuning:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Failure of Empirical Rules (ZN and CC):} Both Ziegler--Nichols and Cohen--Coon formulas prescribed excessively aggressive gains ($K_p \approx -45$ to $-51$), driven by the small apparent dead time ($\theta = 0.093$~min, $r = 0.085$) \cite{odwyer2009handbook}. Upon step initiation, the control command instantly saturated the valve ($q_j = 300$~L/min), severing the linear feedback loop. Although anti-windup clamping halted integrator growth, the high proportional action triggered violent limit cycles with extreme overshoot ($M_p > 800\%$), proving that empirical rules formulated for unconstrained linear delay systems cannot be safely applied to lag-dominant exothermic reactors \cite{shinskey1996process, luyben2007chemical}.
    
    \item \textbf{Superiority of Model-Based and $\mathcal{H}_\infty$-Constrained Control:} Skogestad SIMC achieved smooth, well-damped tracking ($IAE = 1.09$, $M_p = 2.1\%$) with moderate actuator effort ($TV = 20.9$~L/min) and zero valve saturation. The Constrained Optimal ITAE controller ($M_s \le 1.6$) achieved the fastest command tracking ($IAE = 0.89$, $M_p = 4.8\%$) while strictly respecting robustness margins ($M_s = 1.24$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Unmeasured Exothermic Load Disturbance Rejection}
In commercial CSTR operations, unmeasured feed stream perturbations frequently destabilize the thermal balance \cite{seborg2010process, bequette2003process}. To stress-test regulatory disturbance rejection, two severe sequential shocks were injected:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item At $t = 2.0$~min: A feed temperature surge $\Delta T_f = +5.0$~K (representing upstream preheater failure);
    \item At $t = 8.0$~min: An unmeasured $+20\%$ reactant feed concentration spike ($\Delta C_{Af} = +0.20$~mol/L, generating sudden exothermic heat release).
\end{enumerate}

Closed-loop regulatory trajectories are depicted in Fig.~\ref{fig:disturbance_rejection}, with performance metrics compiled in Table~\ref{tab:load_disturbance}.

Under the severe exothermic heat release triggered by the combined disturbances, ZN and CC permitted catastrophic thermal excursions ($\Delta T_{\text{peak}} = +56.5$~K and $+43.3$~K, respectively), pushing the reactor into near-runaway conditions before recovering into persistent limit cycles \cite{uppal1974dynamic, aris1958analysis}. In contrast, both Skogestad SIMC and Constrained Optimal ITAE contained peak temperature deviations to under $7.4$~K, smoothly opening the cooling valve without chatter and rapidly restoring the reactor to setpoint.

\subsection{Thermal Fouling and Physical Controllability Limits}
Heat exchanger surfaces inevitably suffer from scaling and polymer fouling, leading to progressive degradation of the overall heat transfer coefficient $UA$ \cite{luyben2007chemical}. To evaluate parametric robustness, $UA$ was degraded systematically from $100\%$ down to $60\%$ of nominal. Performance metrics across fouling levels are reported in Table~\ref{tab:fouling_sensitivity}, and the trajectory comparison between clean ($100\% UA$) and fouled ($70\% UA$) jackets is displayed in Fig.~\ref{fig:fouling_robustness}.

The findings confirm the analytical controllability boundaries derived in Section~\ref{subsec:thermal_controllability}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item For $UA \ge 70\%$, both Skogestad SIMC and Constrained Optimal ITAE preserve closed-loop stability and zero steady-state offset by automatically increasing the baseline coolant flow rate;
    \item At $60\% UA$, the maximum cooling capacity $Q_{r, \max}(T)$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:max_cooling_limit}) drops below the peak Arrhenius heat generation rate $Q_g(T)$. Even with the cooling valve fully saturated at $q_j = u_{\max} = 300$~L/min, heat removal is physically insufficient, precipitating irreversible thermal runaway. This marks the hard thermodynamic controllability boundary of the physical system.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Authentic Continuous Pareto Frontier and Robustness Limits}
\label{subsec:pareto_results}
To resolve the fundamental trade-off between setpoint tracking accuracy and actuator physical wear, a continuous parameter sweep of the Skogestad SIMC closed-loop time constant $\tau_c \in [0.015, 2.50]$~min (45 logarithmically spaced points) and a scalarized multi-objective optimization across trade-off weights $\lambda \in [0.05, 0.95]$ were conducted. The resulting authentic Pareto frontier in the $(TV, IAE)$ plane and the sensitivity peak response are illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:pareto_iae_tv}, with representative quantitative points tabulated in Table~\ref{tab:pareto_tradeoff}.

\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figures/fig4_pareto_iae_tv.pdf}
    \caption{Authentic continuous Pareto trade-off and sensitivity boundaries: (a) Continuous Pareto frontier in the $(TV, IAE)$ plane mapping the SIMC $\tau_c$-sweep locus, scalarized multi-objective optimal front ($\lambda$-opt), and discrete classical benchmarks, color-coded by maximum sensitivity $M_s$; (b) Variation of maximum sensitivity peak $M_s = \|S\|_\infty$ and tracking error $IAE$ as a function of the closed-loop tuning parameter $\tau_c$ on a logarithmic scale.}
    \label{fig:pareto_iae_tv}
\end{figure*}

\input{tables/pareto_tradeoff_table}

The continuous sweep reveals fundamental design insights:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Non-linear Pareto Front in $(TV, IAE)$:} As depicted in Fig.~\ref{fig:pareto_iae_tv}(a), the trade-off curve exhibits a sharp "knee" around $TV \approx 20\text{--}35$~L/min and $IAE \approx 1.05\text{--}1.10$~K$\cdot$min, corresponding to $\tau_c \in [0.25, 0.40]$~min. Selecting $\tau_c < 0.10$~min produces diminishing returns in tracking speed ($\Delta IAE < 0.15$~K$\cdot$min) while drastically inflating actuator wear ($TV > 60$~L/min) and overshoot ($M_p > 25\%$) \cite{grimholt2018optimal, alcantara2013simple}.
    
    \item \textbf{Robustness Fragility and Sensitivity Peak $M_s$:} Fig.~\ref{fig:pareto_iae_tv}(b) delineates the robustness boundary. When $\tau_c$ is reduced below $0.10$~min, the peak sensitivity $M_s$ rises sharply, exceeding the robust target $M_s = 1.6$ and approaching the stability limit $M_s = 2.0$. This frequency-domain erosion directly correlates with the onset of time-domain actuator saturation and overshoot \cite{garpinger2014performance, padula2012optimal}.
    
    \item \textbf{Mapping Classical Rules on the Design Space:} The discrete classical benchmarks occupy vastly disparate regions of the Pareto space. ZN and CC fall deep into the unstable/saturated quadrant ($TV \approx 480$~L/min, $IAE > 100$~K$\cdot$min), whereas Skogestad SIMC ($\tau_c = 0.39$~min) and Constrained Optimal ITAE align closely with the theoretical multi-objective optimal front ($\lambda$-opt), delivering an ideal compromise between regulation quality and valve longevity \cite{reynoso2021multiobjective}.
\end{itemize}

The multistep evaluation underscores the inherent limitations of fixed-gain linear control: while a single tuned parameter set stabilizes the system around the nominal design point, closed-loop damping and response speed vary significantly between the moderate reaction regime ($390$~K, lower conversion) and the high-temperature regime ($402$~K, where Arrhenius acceleration reduces effective stability margins). This observation provides strong justification for the adaptive neural and supervisory multi-agent architectures developed in subsequent stages of this research.
