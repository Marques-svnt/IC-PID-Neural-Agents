\subsection{Nominal Setpoint Tracking Benchmark}
The four PID tuning strategies were first evaluated under nominal operating conditions for a $-2.0$~K step in reactor temperature setpoint ($T_{sp} = 394.65$~K) under realistic sensor noise ($\sigma = 0.10$~K). The closed-loop state and control trajectories are illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:classical_benchmark}, with quantitative performance metrics summarized in Table~\ref{tab:classical_benchmark}.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig1_classical_benchmark.pdf}
    \caption{Nominal closed-loop setpoint tracking comparison ($-2$~K step) showing reactor temperature $T$ and coolant flow rate $q_j$ under actuator saturation limits.}
    \label{fig:classical_benchmark}
\end{figure}

\input{tables/benchmark_table}

The simulation highlights critical trade-offs in classical tuning:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Failure of Empirical Rules (ZN and CC):} Both Ziegler--Nichols and Cohen--Coon rules produced excessively high gains ($K_p \approx -45$ to $-51$), driven by the small apparent dead time ($\theta = 0.093$~min). Upon the step change, the controllers immediately saturated the valve ($q_j = 300$~L/min), inducing violent limit cycles and unacceptable overshoot ($M_p > 800\%$).
    \item \textbf{Superiority of Model-Based and Optimal Strategies:} Skogestad SIMC achieved smooth, well-damped tracking with $IAE = 1.77$ and $M_p = 12.1\%$, without actuator saturation. The Constrained Optimal ITAE controller ($M_s \le 1.6$) delivered the best overall tracking performance ($IAE = 1.63$, $ITAE = 4.70$, $M_s = 1.22$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Unmeasured Load Disturbance Rejection}
In industrial CSTR operations, unmeasured upstream variations frequently perturb the thermal and kinetic equilibrium. To evaluate disturbance rejection, the reactor was subjected to two sequential unmeasured shocks:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item At $t = 2.0$~min: A feed temperature surge of $\Delta T_f = +5.0$~K.
    \item At $t = 8.0$~min: An unmeasured $+20\%$ feed reactant concentration step ($\Delta C_{Af} = +0.20$~mol/L).
\end{enumerate}

The closed-loop dynamic responses are presented in Fig.~\ref{fig:disturbance_rejection}, and performance metrics are compiled in Table~\ref{tab:load_disturbance}.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig2_disturbance_rejection.pdf}
    \caption{Regulatory response under unmeasured load disturbances: $+5$~K feed temperature surge at $t = 2$~min, followed by $+20\%$ feed concentration spike at $t = 8$~min.}
    \label{fig:disturbance_rejection}
\end{figure}

\input{tables/disturbance_rejection_table}

Under the severe exothermic heat release triggered by the concentration and temperature surges, Ziegler--Nichols and Cohen--Coon permitted extreme temperature spikes ($\Delta T_{\text{peak}} = +56.5$~K and $+43.3$~K, respectively), pushing the reactor toward thermal runaway before recovering into limit cycles. Conversely, both SIMC and Optimal ITAE contained the peak deviation to just $7.3$~K, returning the system swiftly to regulatory setpoint.

\subsection{Thermal Fouling and Parameter Uncertainty}
Jacket heat exchanger surfaces naturally suffer from mineral scaling and polymer fouling over operational lifecycles, causing gradual reduction of the overall heat transfer coefficient $UA$. To quantify robustness against thermal degradation, $UA$ was degraded systematically from $100\%$ down to $60\%$ of its nominal value. The results are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:fouling_sensitivity}, and the trajectory comparison between clean ($100\%$) and severely fouled ($70\%$) conditions is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:fouling_robustness}.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig3_fouling_robustness.pdf}
    \caption{Effect of heat transfer degradation on closed-loop stability: comparison between clean jacket ($100\% UA$) and fouled jacket ($70\% UA$).}

While ZN and CC exhibited severe instability across all fouling levels, SIMC and Constrained Optimal ITAE maintained stable operation and zero steady-state offset down to $70\% UA$. At $60\% UA$, the heat transfer capability is physically insufficient to reject reaction heat without continuous valve saturation, marking the absolute controllability boundary of the process.

\subsection{Sensor Noise Sensitivity and Pareto Trade-off ($IAE \times TV$)}
To quantify actuator wear under measurement noise, a Monte Carlo sensitivity analysis ($5$ independent realizations per noise level) was conducted across noise standard deviations $\sigma \in [0.0, 1.0]$~K. Table~\ref{tab:noise_sensitivity} reports the tracking error ($IAE$) and actuator effort ($TV$), while Fig.~\ref{fig:pareto_iae_tv} maps the resulting Pareto trade-off frontier.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig4_pareto_iae_tv.pdf}
    \caption{Pareto frontier illustrating the multi-objective trade-off between tracking error ($IAE$) and actuator wear ($TV$) across increasing measurement noise levels ($\sigma = 0.0$ to $1.0$~K).}
    \label{fig:pareto_iae_tv}
\end{figure}

\input{tables/noise_chattering_table}

In the noise-free baseline ($\sigma = 0.0$~K), SIMC and Optimal ITAE achieve negligible control effort ($TV = 22.5$~L/min and $46.6$~L/min). As sensor noise intensifies, the derivative filter attenuates high-frequency noise, but $TV$ rises to $\approx 770$~L/min. Controllers with excessive proportional gains (ZN, CC) suffer from severe chattering even under mild noise, causing premature valve packing and seal wear in industrial environments.

\subsection{Multi-Operating Point Tracking across Kinetic Regimes}
Finally, a wide-range multistep setpoint tracking test ($396.65\text{ K} \rightarrow 390.0\text{ K} \rightarrow 402.0\text{ K} \rightarrow 396.65\text{ K}$) was conducted to evaluate non-linear gain variation across different reaction regimes. As plotted in Fig.~\ref{fig:multistep_tracking} and quantified in Table~\ref{tab:multistep_tracking}, the non-linear process gain and time constant vary substantially between the lower conversion regime ($390$~K) and the strongly exothermic runaway-sensitive regime ($402$~K).

    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig5_multistep_tracking.pdf}

The multistep experiment clearly demonstrates the limitations of fixed-gain linear PID controllers: while a single tuned parameter set can stabilize the system at the design point, performance degrades when transitioning across operating regimes. This result provides clear academic justification for the adaptive neural network and agent-based gain scheduling architectures to be investigated in subsequent works.
