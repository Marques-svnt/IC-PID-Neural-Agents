%  Section 1: Introduction
% ============================================================

Proportional-Integral-Derivative (PID) controllers remain the indisputable backbone of industrial automation, regulating over $90\%$ of control loops across the chemical and petrochemical industries \cite{astrom2006pid, seborg2010process}. Despite their widespread adoption, the vast majority of industrial loops operate under sub-optimal or overly aggressive tunings, largely because classical tuning formulas—such as the seminal Ziegler--Nichols reaction curve \cite{ziegler1942optimum} and Cohen--Coon empirical correlations \cite{cohen1953theoretical}—were formulated under idealized assumptions of linear, unconstrained plants \cite{odwyer2009handbook, shinskey1996process}. In the modern process control landscape, deploying fixed-gain controllers on highly non-linear, exothermic chemical reactors poses formidable challenges that cannot be resolved through simplistic quarter-amplitude decay criteria \cite{visioli2006practical, luyben2007chemical}.

Continuous Stirred Tank Reactors (CSTRs) conducting exothermic reactions represent a canonical archetype of process non-linearity \cite{uppal1974dynamic, aris1958analysis}. Arrhenius reaction kinetics introduce an exponential positive feedback loop between reactor core temperature and reaction rate, generating steady-state multiplicity, open-loop thermal instability, and parametric sensitivity \cite{bequette2003process, rawlings2020model}. In real-world installations, this thermodynamic fragility is compounded by hard physical constraints on the final control elements: cooling control valves exhibit finite amplitude bounds ($[u_{\min}, u_{\max}]$) and rate-of-change (slew-rate) limits imposed by actuator mechanics \cite{hippe2006windup, tarbouriech2009antiwindup}. When an aggressive linear controller drives the actuator into prolonged saturation, the linear feedback loop is temporarily severed, inducing catastrophic integrator windup, severe limit cycles, or unrecoverable thermal runaway \cite{bohn1995analysis, peng1996nonlinear}.

While contemporary literature in robust process control has extensively analyzed model-based architectures—such as Skogestad's Simple Internal Model Control (SIMC) \cite{skogestad2003simple, grimholt2018optimal} and $\mathcal{H}_\infty$-constrained optimization \cite{garpinger2014performance, padula2012optimal}—there remains an acute need for a rigorous, systematic benchmark quantifying the exact physical controllability boundaries of classical PID control under coupled non-linearities. In particular, existing studies frequently treat the failure of empirical rules as an isolated curiosity or report performance trade-offs under arbitrary stochastic noise variations, rather than mapping the authentic continuous Pareto frontier defined by control design parameters and $\mathcal{H}_\infty$ sensitivity limits \cite{alcantara2013simple, reynoso2021multiobjective}.

To establish an authoritative, reproducible baseline for process control researchers and provide a benchmark against which upcoming adaptive neural and supervisory multi-agent architectures can be validated, this paper investigates the physical limits of fixed-gain PID control on a non-isothermal CSTR. The core contributions of this work are fourfold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{High-Fidelity Process and Controllability Formulation:} We formulate a coupled three-state non-linear CSTR model featuring dynamic jacket enthalpy balances, Arrhenius kinetics, and physical valve limits (amplitude and slew rate), deriving analytical thermal controllability boundaries via Van Heerden heat generation/removal balances;
    \item \textbf{Comprehensive Benchmarking Framework:} We evaluate empirical reaction-curve rules (Ziegler--Nichols, Cohen--Coon) against model-based robust control (Skogestad SIMC) and constrained numerical optimization (ITAE with an explicit $\mathcal{H}_\infty$ peak sensitivity constraint $M_s \le 1.6$), elucidating the role of integrator anti-windup mechanics;
    \item \textbf{Parametric Stress and Fouling Robustness:} We subject the closed-loop architectures to unmeasured exothermic load disturbances (feed temperature and concentration surges) and systematic heat exchanger surface fouling ($UA$ degradation from $100\%$ down to the physical controllability threshold of $60\%$), delineating the operational boundary where linear control authority fails;
    \item \textbf{Continuous Pareto Frontier and Sensitivity Sweep:} We construct the authentic Pareto frontier between setpoint tracking performance ($\text{IAE}$) and actuator physical wear ($\text{TV}$) through a continuous sweep of the closed-loop design time constant $\tau_c$ and scalarized multi-objective optimization, demonstrating how $\mathcal{H}_\infty$ sensitivity constraints dictate the boundary between high performance and actuator degradation.
\end{enumerate}
